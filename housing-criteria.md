# What I'm Looking For — SF Rental Criteria

A cheat sheet of my apartment hunt filters, so you know what to send my way (and what to skip).
If a listing clears **Price + Neighborhood + the four must-haves**, send it. Everything else is nice-to-have.

---

## The Short Version

> **Studio / 1BR / room / in-law in San Francisco, under $2,000/mo, north-and-central neighborhoods only, with a private bathroom, a kitchen in the unit, and laundry in the unit or building.**

If you only remember one line, that's it.

## 3. The Four Must-Haves (all non-negotiable)

1. **Private bathroom.** No shared baths, ever — no matter how good the price or location. For a room in a shared house, the listing has to explicitly say *private bath* or *en-suite*. Silence on the subject counts as a no.
2. **Kitchen in the unit.** Full kitchen or at minimum a kitchenette. A shared/communal kitchen does not count.
3. **Laundry in-unit or on-site.** Washer/dryer in the unit, or shared laundry in the building. "Laundromat down the street" does not count.
4. **Accepted neighborhood** (section 2), confirmed — not just claimed.

**Ambiguity is a rejection.** If a listing doesn't mention the bathroom, the kitchen, or the laundry, I treat that as a no rather than something to chase down. There are enough listings.

### Acceptable unit types
Studios · 1-bedrooms · rooms in shared housing (with private bath) · in-law units / ADUs / backyard cottages / basement apartments

---

## 4. Income-Restricted Housing — Automatic No

My income is over $100k/year, which disqualifies me from most below-market-rate programs. So:

- **Skip anything whose max income cap for a 1-person household is under $100,000/year.** That rules out most BMR / MOHCD / AMI-tier units (30%, 50%, 60%, 80% AMI), Mercy Housing, Brightwell, Madonna Residences, and similar.
- 120% AMI or higher *might* work — but only if the listing explicitly states a 1-person cap above $100k.
- If it's income-restricted and doesn't publish the cap: skip it. Assume it disqualifies.

This one catches a lot of otherwise-perfect-looking cheap listings, so it's worth checking early.

## 5. Nice-to-Haves (tracked, never required)

Parking (included, paid, or even just street) · dishwasher · air conditioning · pet-friendly · furnished · natural light or outdoor space

None of these make or break anything — they're tiebreakers.

## 6. Scam Filters — What to Ignore

Roughly **half** of everything this search has ever surfaced disappeared within 24 hours, most of it flagged as fraudulent. The SF listing market is heavily polluted by batch-posting scammers, so a listing that looks too good almost always is.

**Skip on sight if you see:**

- **Price way below market** — 30%+ under typical for that neighborhood and unit type. This is the single most reliable scam tell, not a deal signal.
- **No photos, or 1–2 generic/stock photos**
- **The same photos or body text reposted** under different accounts or multiple simultaneous URLs
- **"Text me your phone number" / "send me your email" closings** with no address, no cross-streets, no landlord or property manager name
- **Upfront fees before a viewing** — application fee + "admin holding fee" combos ($35 app + $200 holding is the classic), especially paid to a personal Gmail rather than a real screening service (SmartMove, RentSpree, AppFolio)
- **Weird license number formatting** — real CA DRE numbers are 8 digits, unspaced. Things like `02.04.48.14` or `0 07 66 41 2` are fabricated. Out-of-state licenses (Illinois, Texas) on an SF listing are the same story.
- **Landlord is "overseas,"** wants a wire transfer, or refuses a live video call / in-person showing while offering a pre-recorded "virtual tour" — the "respecting the current tenant's privacy" line is a scripted tell
- **Body text that doesn't match the listing** — a "1BR" describing 5 bedrooms, or copy pasted from an out-of-state property (Florida zip codes, "snow removal," a Miami condo)
- **A batch of listings from one poster** across many different neighborhoods at once, all with identical amenity lists and the same 3-day consecutive open house window

If a listing is genuinely great and genuinely real, it'll have a specific address, a named property manager, a plausible price, and normal photos.

## TL;DR for Forwarding Me Things

**Send it if:** SF, under $2k, in one of the yes-neighborhoods, private bath, own kitchen, laundry in the unit or building.

**Don't bother if:** shared bathroom · Sunset/Bayview/Bernal/Excelsior/Tenderloin · below-market-rate or income-capped · the price seems unreal · the listing wants your phone number before it'll tell you the address.
